This study examines whether the principal player's performance against the selected opponent differs according to whether the principal player directly precedes that opponent in the game's initial turn order.

Qualifying Games

The analysis uses the same qualifying five-participant games as the starting-position study: two selected human players and three computer players, with valid turn-order positions. Each game contributes one observation.

Circular Turn Order

The positions form a circle: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 1. The principal player directly precedes the opponent when the opponent occupies the next position in this sequence. For example, principal position 5 and opponent position 1 count as directly preceding.

Directly Precedes
The opponent is immediately next in the circular order.
Does Not Precede
The opponent is not immediately next in the circular order.
Win Rate
Wins divided by games; ties count as non-wins.
Average Score
Mean final score of the principal player.
Score Differential
Principal score minus opponent score for each game.

Research Question

Does win probability differ when the principal player directly precedes the opponent?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Win probability is the same in both groups.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Win probability differs between groups.

Method

A two-sided Fisher's exact test compares wins and non-wins (losses plus ties) between the two groups. The reported odds ratio is the odds of winning in directly-preceding games divided by the odds in other games.

Interpretation

An odds ratio above 1 favors the directly-preceding group; below 1 favors the other group. The p-value evaluates evidence against equal win probabilities under the test model. An odds ratio is not a difference in win percentages.

Research Question

Does the principal player's average final score differ between the two groups?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Mean final scores are equal.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Mean final scores differ.

Method

A two-sided Monte Carlo permutation test compares the observed difference in group means with differences obtained by randomly reallocating the recorded scores between groups, preserving their original sample sizes.

Interpretation

The reported statistic is the directly-precedes mean minus the does-not-precede mean, in points. A positive value indicates higher average scoring in the directly-preceding group.

Research Question

Does the principal player's average score differential against the opponent differ between groups?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Mean score differentials are equal.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): Mean score differentials differ.

Method

For each game, calculate principal score minus opponent score. Apply the same two-sided two-sample mean permutation test to these game-level differentials.

Interpretation

The reported statistic is the difference between the groups' average score differentials. Positive values indicate a relatively better scoring margin when directly preceding.

Statistical Evidence

The significance level is 0.05. A p-value below 0.05 is treated as statistically significant. A nonsignificant result does not prove the groups are equivalent.

H2b and H2c use 100,000 Monte Carlo permutations and random seed 42 by default. They compare absolute differences in means for a two-sided test, assuming outcomes are exchangeable between the groups under the null model.

Important Limitations
  • Unequal or small group sizes can limit statistical power.
  • Permutation inference depends on exchangeability; unequal score variances can complicate interpretation as a test of equal means alone.
  • These three exploratory p-values are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.
  • Observed associations do not establish causation or control for all game conditions.
  • The grouping uses the initial turn order; the starting player changes between rounds in Wingspan.

Interpret p-values together with sample sizes, observed differences, and the study assumptions.
