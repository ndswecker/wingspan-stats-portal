This study examines whether a player's starting position is associated with their performance against a selected opponent.

Qualifying Games

Only competitive games containing both selected human players are included. The study assumes five participants: two humans and three computer players. Each qualifying game contributes one observation.

Variables
Principal Player
The player whose performance is being studied.
Opponent
The human player against whom performance is compared.
Starting Position
The principal player's initial turn-order position, numbered 1 through 5.
Games
Number of qualifying games in a position.
Wins / Losses / Ties
Outcomes determined by comparing the two human players' final scores.
Win Rate
Wins divided by total games in that position. Ties count as non-wins.
Average Score
Mean final score of the principal player for games in that position.
Average Differential
Mean of principal score minus opponent score. Positive values favor the principal player; negative values favor the opponent.

Starting position is categorical. The study does not assume that its effect increases or decreases progressively from position 1 to 5.

Research Question

Does the principal player's probability of winning differ across starting positions?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Win probability is equal across all five positions.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one position has a different win probability.

Method

Games are classified as wins or non-wins. Both losses and ties are non-wins.

A Pearson chi-square statistic measures how much the observed win and non-win counts differ from the counts expected if win probability were independent of starting position.

Permutation testing evaluates how unusual that statistic is under the null hypothesis.

Interpretation

A larger chi-square statistic indicates greater disagreement between observed and expected outcomes. The p-value determines whether this disagreement is unusual under the permutation null model.

This test evaluates whether win probability differs somewhere among the positions. It does not identify which position is responsible or establish that starting position causes a competitive advantage.

Research Question

Does the principal player's mean final score differ across starting positions?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Mean final scores are equal across all five positions.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one position has a different mean score.

Method

A one-way ANOVA F-statistic compares variation between the position means against variation within the positions.

A permutation test evaluates the observed F-statistic against statistics obtained by randomly reassigning observed scores among the starting-position groups.

Interpretation

A larger F-statistic indicates that differences between group means are large relative to variation within groups.

This test concerns scoring performance, not winning. A player may score more points from a position without necessarily winning more often from that position.

Research Question

Does the principal player's mean score differential differ across starting positions?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Mean score differentials are equal across positions.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one position has a different mean score differential.

Method

For each game, score differential is calculated:

Principal Score − Opponent Score

A one-way ANOVA F-statistic compares mean differentials across positions. Permutation testing evaluates the observed statistic against randomly reassigned differentials.

Interpretation

A positive mean differential indicates that the principal player outscored the opponent on average. A negative value indicates the opposite.

Unlike H1a, this analysis considers the magnitude of scoring differences, not simply whether the principal player won.

Permutation Testing

Under the null hypothesis, performance outcomes are assumed exchangeable across starting-position groups. The analysis repeatedly shuffles outcomes while retaining group sizes.

The observed test statistic is compared with the statistics produced by these shuffled datasets. The proportion of permutations producing equally or more extreme statistics provides an estimated p-value.

Statistical Variables
Test Statistic
A numerical summary of the observed differences between groups. H1a uses chi-square; H1b and H1c use F-statistics.
P-value
The estimated probability, under the null hypothesis and permutation procedure, of obtaining a test statistic at least as extreme as the observed statistic.
Significance Level
The threshold used for evaluating evidence. This study uses 0.05 (5%).
Significant
The p-value is below 0.05, providing evidence against the specified null hypothesis under the test assumptions.
Not Significant
The p-value is not below 0.05. This does not establish that no association exists.
Important Limitations
  • Starting positions may have different numbers of qualifying games.
  • Small samples can produce unstable averages and limit the ability to detect differences.
  • The three hypotheses represent multiple exploratory tests. Their individual p-values are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.
  • The analysis assumes a five-player game with two humans and three computer players.
  • Starting position is a simplified measure; Wingspan's starting player changes between rounds.
  • The analysis does not establish causation or control for every possible influence on player performance.

Statistical results should be interpreted alongside the observed differences, sample sizes, study assumptions, and limitations. The researcher is responsible for evaluating the evidence.
