This study groups games by the number of computer players encountered when moving forward from the principal player to the opponent in the initial circular five-position turn order.

Only qualifying games with the two selected human players and three NPCs are included. Each game contributes one observation. The turn order wraps from position 5 back to position 1.

0 intervening NPCs
The principal player directly precedes the opponent (same group as H2).
1, 2, or 3 NPCs
That many NPC turns occur before the opponent's next turn.
Win Rate
Wins divided by all games in the category; ties count as non-wins.
Average Score
Principal player's mean final score.
Score Differential
Principal score minus opponent score, averaged within each category.

Spacing is treated as a four-category variable, not as a linear predictor. Categories with no games remain visible in the results but are excluded from hypothesis testing.

Research Question

Does the principal player's probability of winning differ among NPC-spacing categories?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Win probability is the same across all represented spacing categories.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one category has a different win probability.

Method

A Pearson chi-square statistic compares wins versus non-wins across spacing categories. The p-value comes from permuting win/non-win outcomes while preserving category sizes. Ties are non-wins.

The chi-square statistic measures association, not the direction of an effect. Compare category win rates for descriptive direction.

Research Question

Does the principal player's average final score differ across NPC-spacing categories?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Mean final scores are equal across categories.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one category has a different mean score.

Method

A one-way ANOVA F-statistic is calculated from individual game scores. Permutation testing reallocates observed scores among categories while preserving their sample sizes.

The F-statistic measures between-group variation relative to within-group variation; it does not identify which category differs or establish a trend.

Research Question

Does the mean score margin against the opponent differ across NPC-spacing categories?

Null Hypothesis (H0): Mean score differentials are equal across categories.

Alternative Hypothesis (H1): At least one category has a different mean differential.

Method

For each game, subtract the opponent's score from the principal player's score. Apply a one-way ANOVA F-statistic and permutation test to these individual game-level margins.

Positive average margins favor the principal player. The omnibus F-test does not identify a specific pair of categories as different.

The significance threshold is α = 0.05. Each test uses 100,000 Monte Carlo permutations and random seed 42. A p-value below 0.05 is classified as statistically significant; a higher p-value is not proof of no effect.

  • Small or uneven category sizes reduce statistical power.
  • Permutation inference assumes observations are exchangeable under the null model; unequal score variability can complicate mean comparisons.
  • These exploratory p-values are not adjusted for multiple comparisons.
  • Games between the same two players may not be fully independent.
  • Associations do not establish causation, and the analysis does not control for other game conditions.
  • Spacing is based on the initial turn order; the starting player changes between rounds.

Interpret p-values alongside sample sizes, observed averages, and the study assumptions.
